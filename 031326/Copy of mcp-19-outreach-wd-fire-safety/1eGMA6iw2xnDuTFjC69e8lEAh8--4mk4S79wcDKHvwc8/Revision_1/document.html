Purpose

The purpose of this working group is to provide maker organizations information and coordinate resources that aids those organizations in providing their membership a safe environment.

Outreach: How Can NOM Help?

Based on a local meeting in Oakland[1] hosted by Omni Commons after the Ghost Ship tragedy a recommended set of action items was identified by those in attendance to help in the Bay Area, this Safety working group put together the following diagram to help with communicating a context for what we are wanting to accomplish and to focus efforts on immediate versus long-term activities:

The Safety Working Group has identified five key stages of the lifecycle of spaces and their needs for safety assistance[2]:

  1. Pre-Construction Education: “What sort of space should I rent/buy?”
  2. Pre-Opening: “Do we have all our safety fundamentals complete?”
  3. In Operation:  “When was the last Safety Check? Who performed it?”
  4. Immediate Needs: “Oh, no! The Fire Marshal is coming. Let’s re-check things.“
  5. Long-Term: “I want to change how I’m using my space.” [d]

For the next month (target end date of 1/12/2017) the Safety Working Group will focus on any Advocacy, Aid Networks, Fundraising and Information recommendations for spaces in the Immediate Need stage.  Assumptions as we targeted our communication and focus on what to recommend/ask in the table below:

  • CAN NOT HELP: We can't really help people using a space in an illegal way, and are getting kicked out by the landlord because of it.
  • CAN HELP A LITTLE: Someone using a space in an illegal way, who wants to get legal, and got a violation from the City. Might be too hard and take a lot more time to help since it may take changes to zoning and / or it is tied to their lease.
  • CAN HELP THE MOST:  Someone using the space they have in a legal way, and need help doing so.  For example, they have a commercial space, using for Making, don't live in it, but had no idea that they had to follow certain fire codes and now are in trouble.[e][f][g][h]

For this next month (target end date of 1/12/2017) the Safety Working Group will focus on helping spaces with Immediate Needs as follows:

Reference Guide

After a quick perusal of the original Problem Statements I found the following related to Risk Management in the area of safety and training:

  1. It is difficult for each space to create material for the training and tracking of member safety instruction or certifications.
  2. Members of a space require clear, consistent training and documentation on key areas such as safety training, operational guides/best practices for key equipment.
  3. Spaces often face challenge to figure out what laws, ordinances, zoning rules, or other guidelines apply to them.
  4. Housekeeping is an ongoing challenge for spaces as members’ projects are often not cleaned up while in process or at the completion, occupying space and tools that others could be using.
  5. Many Makerspaces have limited resources or knowledge to dispose of wastes such as e-waste, hazardous chemicals, or biological, or other non-standard waste.

APPENDIX III:  Discussion on “The Codes”

Capture of the highlights of a discussion in Slack on December 12th, 2016 with Jeffrey McGrew on the different codes:

  • National, Industry or International Code Standards come out every three years.
  • Building Code, Fire Code, Electrical Code, and those sorts of 'how it gets built' codes are all based upon International Codes.
  • These International Code Standards get adopted into the State Code, which then gets additional modifications from the county or city to be the ‘local code’.
  • Another complexity is Zoning / Planning Code.
  • “How It’s Built” can be generalized, but “What Can Go Where” can’t be generalized.

------------- BEGINS HERE------------

In response to the recent Ghost Ship tragedy in Oakland and news of crackdowns on DIY spaces across the country, a group of individuals within the Nation of Makers #Risk _Management Slack channel initiated a Safety Working Group to address any outreach needed in the NoM community.  This Working Group has set out to provide maker and artist organizers support through information and coordination that aid in providing their community and environment that meets local fire and safety code.

[o][p][q][r]

The Safety Working Group has identified three key stages of the lifecycle of spaces and their needs for safety assistance:

  1. Pre-construction Education “What sort of space should I rent/buy?”
  2. Immediate Needs “Oh no, the fire marshal is coming!”
  3. Long-Term “Let's turn this DIY space legal.”

Due to the concerns and urgency to protect existing spaces that are at risk due to the Ghost Ship fire, the Safety Working Group will focus on the following deliverables over the next month (shown in orange in the diagram above):

  • Quick reference guide for space leaders on how to interface with various authorities
  • Quick reference guide for authorities and property owners advocating and indicating what makerspaces are
  • List of potential outside funding resources since NOM is unable to collect or distribute donations as a 5013c at this time.
  • Quick reference guide on Fire Safety Fundamentals

In the spirit of lending a hand, if you know of maker or artist spaces in your community that could be struggling with safety hazards or code violations, we encourage you to reach out to them to relay this information and offer what help you can.  Through our collective insight, pooled resources, and the sheer expanse of our network-the community built here within NOM has the immense capacity to help spaces in need!

As we finish with these Immediate Needs to help spaces, we invite others to join us in providing safety assistance to address Pre-Construction and Long-Term needs.  For anyone looking to get involved, provide feedback, or ask questions, please reach out to us!
